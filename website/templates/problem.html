What is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome?

Carpal tunnel syndrome is one of the most common hand conditions in the world. When pressure is applied on the median nerve in the carpal tunnel of the wrist, the condition starts to develop. The carpal tunnel is located on the palm side of the hand, and it is a narrow passageway that is surrounded by bones and ligaments. When the median nerve is compressed, the carpal tunnel experiences more pressure and narrowing, while leads to the development of carpal tunnel syndrome.

What Symptoms are Associated With the Condition?

There are many symptoms associated with carpal tunnel, but there are three main symptoms that are associated with carpal tunnel syndrome. These symptoms all occur around the wrist area, and they are:

  • Tingling
  • Numbness
  • Weakness

Tingling and numbness are the first symptoms that arrive, and the thumb, middle, and ring fingers are usually affected. It is also possible that you might feel an electric shock in these fingers, and the sensation can also travel up the wrist into the arm. Weakness comes next, and it can cause you to drop objects and struggle to grip objects frequently.

How is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome Treated?

If carpal tunnel syndrome isn't caught early, more extreme methods like surgery, wrist splinting, and anti-inflammatory drugs must be used to treat the condition. However, if the condition is caught before it gets worse, simple things can be done to fix the condition like breaks, calm activities, and cold packs. The problem is that most people are unaware of the dangers of carpal tunnel syndrome and just pass off symptoms as simple muscle pulls or fatigue.
