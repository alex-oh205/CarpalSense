The Problem


What is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome?

Carpal tunnel syndrome is one of the most common hand conditions in the world. The carpal tunnel is located at the wrist on the palm side of the hand, and it is a narrow passageway that is surrounded by bones and ligaments. When the median nerve is compressed, the carpal tunnel experiences more pressure and narrowing, while leads to the development of carpal tunnel syndrome.


What Symptoms are Associated With the Condition?


Tingling

Tingling is one of the first symptoms that occurs during carpal tunnel syndrome. The thumb, middle, and ring fingers are usually affected. You might feel a "pins and needles" sensation and minor discomfort.

Numbness

Numbness usually occurs alongside tingling, and can become worse at night. It is also possible that you might feel a shooting pain in your fingers, and the sensation can travel up the wrist into the arm.

Weakness

Weakness is usually one of the later symptoms of the condition. It can cause you to frequently drop objects and struggle to grip things. You might have difficulty performing precise tasks like buttoning a shirt.

How is Carpal Tunnel Syndrome Treated?

If carpal tunnel syndrome isn't caught early, more extreme methods like surgery, wrist splinting, and anti-inflammatory drugs must be used to treat the condition. However, if the condition is caught before it gets worse, simple things can be done to fix the condition like breaks, calm activities, and cold packs. The problem is that most people are unaware of the dangers of carpal tunnel syndrome and just pass off symptoms as simple muscle pulls or fatigue.
